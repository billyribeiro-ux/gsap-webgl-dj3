Easing & Timing — The Soul of Motion

Easing is the accent of motion — the single choice that makes the difference between "a thing moved" and "a thing arrived." Let's train your eye to read curves, and your hand to pick them.

An easing curve answers one question: how does progress map to time? A linear ease covers equal distance every frame — mechanical, lifeless. A good ease front-loads or back-loads the motion so it accelerates and settles like a physical object. You met this in the principles lesson; now we go deep enough that you can pick the right curve by instinct.

The gallery — read the feel

Same distance, same duration, six different curves. Watch them together until you can feel the difference with your eyes closed. That intuition is worth more than memorizing any number.

Reading a cubic-bezier

cubic-bezier(x1, y1, x2, y2) defines two control handles pulling a curve between (0,0) and (1,1). The x-axis is time, the y-axis is progress.

  • Handle pulled right and up early (like 0.16, 1) → the curve shoots up fast then flattens → fast start, soft landing (expo-out). The premium default for things arriving.
  • y greater than 1 → the curve overshoots past the target and comes back → a spring / overshoot. Energy, playfulness.
  • y below 0 at the start → it dips backward before going forward → anticipation, a wind-up before the move.
Direction picks the curve. Entering elements use an out ease (decelerate into place). Leaving elements use an in ease (accelerate away). Movement between two on-screen states uses in-out. Get just this right and your work immediately reads as considered.

Timing: the other half

Curve is how; duration is how long. A useful scale: micro-feedback (hovers, taps) 100–200ms; standard transitions 200–400ms; entrances and larger moves 400–600ms; grand, hero-scale reveals 600–1000ms. Two rules keep you honest: bigger or farther-travelling things get a touch longer, and when unsure, go faster — users read speed as quality. GSAP, Svelte's Tween and CSS all take these same easing + duration ideas; the vocabulary transfers everywhere.
