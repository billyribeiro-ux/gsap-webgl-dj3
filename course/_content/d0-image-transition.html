The most cinematic way to move between two visuals isn't a crossfade — it's a displacement transition: the outgoing image dissolves into the incoming one along an organic, noise-distorted boundary, with the pixels near that boundary stretching and splitting into colour. This is the engine behind those liquid WebGL carousels and full-page route transitions on studio sites.

The recipe

  • A moving threshold. A uProgress uniform (0→1) sweeps a cutoff line across the surface. Below the line you see image B, above it image A. Drive progress and the wipe travels.
  • Distort the boundary with noise. Instead of a straight line, add value noise to the per-pixel comparison (vUv.y + noise). Now the seam is irregular and organic — the difference between a PowerPoint wipe and something that looks alive.
  • Displace + split at the seam. Compute an edge mask that's strongest right at the boundary, and use it to push pixels around and offset the red/blue channels there. That molten, glowing tear is what sells it.
For real images, swap two lines. Replace the procedural sceneA()/sceneB() with texture2D(uTexA, uv) and texture2D(uTexB, uv), and load your two images as textures. Better still, drive the threshold with a dedicated displacement map (a greyscale texture) instead of procedural noise — that's how designers art-direct the shape of the dissolve (diagonal, circular, ink-blot). Animate uProgress with GSAP and you can scrub the transition to scroll or a button.

Chain these and you have a slideshow where every slide melts into the next; tie uProgress to route changes and your whole site transitions like a film reel. It's the same threshold-plus-noise idea as the glitch and the metaballs — once it clicks, a huge swathe of "how did they do that" WebGL is yours.
