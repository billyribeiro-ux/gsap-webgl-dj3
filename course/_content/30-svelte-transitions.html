Here's Svelte's superpower for motion: it knows when an element enters and leaves the DOM, so it can animate those moments for you. Add transition:fly to an element and Svelte plays it in on mount and reverses it on unmount — automatically, with cleanup handled. This is the thing that's genuinely painful in React and trivial here.

Transitions + FLIP, together

Click Add notification. New toasts fly up and fade in; after a moment they fade out; and crucially, the survivors glide into their new positions instead of jumping. That gliding is FLIP, and Svelte gives it to you with a single directive: animate:flip.

The four directives you'll use forever

  • transition: — one effect used for both enter and leave (it simply reverses on the way out). Great for symmetric motion.
  • in: and out: — separate enter and leave effects. In the demo, toasts fly in but only fade out — that asymmetry feels natural because arrivals deserve more fanfare than exits.
  • animate: — plays when an element's position in a keyed {#each} changes. This is FLIP, and it's how you animate reordering, sorting and removal in lists.
FLIP needs a key. animate:flip only works inside a keyed each block — {#each toasts as t (t.id)}. The key is how Svelte tracks which DOM node is which across updates, so it can measure where each item was (First), where it's going (Last), Invert the difference, and Play it back. That's literally what FLIP stands for.

When built-ins, when GSAP?

Reach for Svelte's transitions first — they're free, accessible and require zero setup for the 90% case: modals, toasts, dropdowns, list changes, page sections. Step up to GSAP (next module) when you need to sequence many things on a shared timeline, scrub animation to scroll, or split and animate text. The two coexist beautifully: Svelte for state-driven enter/leave, GSAP for choreography.

There's also svelte/motion — Tween and Spring — for smoothly animating numbers (a counter, a progress bar, a dragged value). We cover those in the next lesson.
