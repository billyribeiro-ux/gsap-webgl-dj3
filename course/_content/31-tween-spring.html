Svelte's svelte/motion module gives you two reactive wrappers for values that should change smoothly rather than instantly. You set a target; the wrapper eases its current value toward it, and your template just reads that current value. They're the cleanest way to animate anything numeric.

  • Tween — duration-based, like a CSS transition but for any value. Great for counters, progress bars and predictable timing.
  • Spring — physics-based. No fixed duration; it settles according to stiffness and damping. Perfect for things that should feel alive and be interruptible — drags, cursor-followers, toggles.

Spring physics you can feel

Move your pointer across the pad — the orb chases it with spring physics, smoothly redirecting mid-flight. Then drag the stiffness and damping sliders and feel the personality change: stiff + low damping is snappy and bouncy; soft + high damping is heavy and calm.

Why this beats hand-rolled animation

Notice the API: coords.target = {`{ x, y }`} to aim, and read coords.current in the template. That's it. The Spring runs its own animation loop, batches updates, and — crucially — interrupts gracefully. If the target changes mid-animation (as it does every time your pointer moves), it doesn't restart from zero; it smoothly curves toward the new target, carrying its existing momentum. Reproducing that by hand with requestAnimationFrame is genuinely tricky. Here it's free.

Tween for telling, Spring for feeling. Use a Tween when the timing must be exact and repeatable — a stat counting up to 1,284, a loading bar, a value that animates over precisely 600ms with a chosen easing. Use a Spring when the motion should respond to the user and feel physical. Both also expose .target and .current, so you can even bind an <input> straight to them — which is how those sliders re-tune the spring live.

Bonus: animating a counter with Tween

A Tween isn't limited to positions. const stat = new Tween(0, { duration: 800, easing: cubicOut }), then set stat.target = 1284 when the section scrolls into view, and render {`{Math.round(stat.current)}`}. That single pattern powers every "numbers ticking up" stats band you've ever seen — and it respects reduced-motion automatically when you pair it with prefersReducedMotion from the same module.
