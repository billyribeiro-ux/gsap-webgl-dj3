This is the summit. A real fluid solver runs the same physics as a wind tunnel — the Navier-Stokes equations — discretized onto a grid and stepped each frame. Jos Stam's "stable fluids" method makes it tractable in real time, and WebGPU's compute passes map onto it perfectly: one pass per stage of the solve. Move your cursor to push the fluid; let go and watch it billow on its own.

The solve, pass by pass

  1. Advect — move the velocity field through itself. For each cell we trace backwards along the velocity and bilinearly sample where the fluid came from. This is the "carry the fluid along" step.
  2. Add forces — your cursor injects velocity (and coloured dye) with a soft Gaussian splat.
  3. Project (the hard part) — real fluids are incompressible: nothing piles up. We compute the field's divergence, solve a Poisson equation for pressure with ~20 Jacobi iterations (each a compute pass reading its neighbours), then subtract the pressure gradient to remove all divergence. This projection is what makes fluid swirl instead of just smear.
  4. Advect dye — carry the colour through the now divergence-free velocity field, and render it with a tone-map.
Pressure projection is the soul of fluid. Skip it and you get smoke that smears lifelessly; include it and the fluid curls, eddies and conserves momentum like the real thing. It's an iterative solve — more Jacobi iterations = more accurate incompressibility = more cost — so you trade quality for speed. Everything here is grid-based compute: a dozen passes ping-ponging a handful of buffers.
