For forty years, real-time 3D meant triangles. Then in 2023, 3D Gaussian Splatting changed the conversation: represent a scene as a cloud of anisotropic 3D Gaussians — little fuzzy, coloured ellipsoids — and rasterize them as soft blobs. Fit a few million to a set of photographs and you can fly through a photoreal capture of the real world in your browser. It's the hottest topic in graphics, and the rendering primitive is surprisingly approachable.

How a 3D Gaussian becomes a pixel

  • Each splat is an ellipsoid. It has a centre, a colour, an opacity, and a 3×3 covariance — its size and orientation in space (here we store it as three scaled principal axes). Anisotropy is the magic: a splat can be a long thin sliver that hugs a surface, so a handful cover a wide area smoothly.
  • Projection: 3D covariance → 2D ellipse (EWA splatting). To draw one, we project its 3D shape into a screen-space ellipse. We do it with a finite-difference trick — project the centre and the three axis-tips, and build the 2D covariance from how far each axis moved on screen — then eigen-decompose that 2×2 to get the ellipse's major and minor axes. The real paper uses the analytic Jacobian of the projection; the idea is identical.
  • Rasterize a soft billboard. We draw a quad oriented to that ellipse, and the fragment shader's alpha is a Gaussian falloff, exp(-½·d²). Thousands of these, blended together, reconstruct a continuous surface.
The two hard parts in production. First, sorting: correct alpha compositing needs splats drawn back-to-front, so real renderers GPU-radix-sort millions of them every frame. (We use additive blending here, which is order-independent and gives a luminous look, to keep the demo simple — that's the honest shortcut.) Second, fitting: the Gaussians aren't authored, they're optimized by gradient descent to match input photos — differentiable rendering, the same machinery as neural networks. You've now built the renderer half of the most important 3D technique of the decade. The frontier isn't somewhere else — you're standing on it.
