Audio-reactive visuals are the heartbeat of music sites, live-stream overlays and generative art. The bridge is the AnalyserNode: connect it into your audio graph and each frame it gives you an array of frequency magnitudes — bass in the low bins, highs at the top. Push that array into a texture and a fragment shader can read it per-pixel. Hit play (we synthesize the sound, so there's nothing to download).

From soundwave to shader

  • The analyser. ctx.createAnalyser() with fftSize = 256 yields 128 frequency bins. Call getByteFrequencyData(array) once per frame to fill it with 0–255 magnitudes. Averaging the lowest few bins gives you a clean bass value — the single most useful signal for "pump on the beat."
  • The data texture. Copy those 128 bytes into a DataTexture (128×1) and mark it needsUpdate. Now the GPU can sample the spectrum: in the shader we map a pixel's angle to a bin and read its magnitude, drawing a radial bar that long.
  • The reaction. Bar length comes from the per-angle frequency; the glowing core scales with bass. Two uniforms — a spectrum texture and a bass float — and the whole scene dances. Swap the shader and the same data could drive particles, displacement, or colour.
Autoplay is blocked — and that's correct. Browsers won't let audio start without a user gesture, so everything kicks off on the Play click (which also resume()s the AudioContext). Always provide that explicit control, keep the volume gentle, and tear the graph down on unmount — an AudioContext left open keeps the audio hardware awake and drains battery. For real tracks, swap the synthesized oscillators for a <audio> element routed through createMediaElementSource(), or a microphone via getUserMedia for a live visualizer.

You now have the full signal chain: synthesize or load sound → analyse it → texture → shader. That's the same pipeline behind festival visuals and generative album art, running in a browser tab at 60fps.
