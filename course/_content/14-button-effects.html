A great button rewards the pointer. It doesn't just change color — it responds. In this lesson we build a small library of reusable button effects, ordered from dead-simple to genuinely advanced, all sharing one base class. Every one is in the live panel; hover them, click the last one, then copy the whole set.

The cookbook — hover them all

Ten effects, one .btn base. The first is the classic fill-up; by the end you've got a pointer-following magnetic button and one that bursts particles on click.

Simple → advanced, and how each works

  1. Fill up — a ::before layer with transform: scaleY(0) and transform-origin: bottom scales to 1 on hover. Animating transform (not height) keeps it GPU-smooth. The whole effect is six lines.
  2. Slide fill — identical idea, but scaleX from the left. Change the origin, change the direction.
  3. Border draw — two pseudo-elements, each drawing one corner's two sides by transitioning width/height from 0 to 100% with staggered delays, so the outline appears to draw itself in.
  4. Shine — a skewed white gradient (::before) slides across with translateX. The premium "gloss" you see on app-store buttons.
  5. Label swap — two stacked spans in a grid cell; on hover the first slides up and out while the second slides in. Great for "Hover me → Let's go."
  6. Icon reveal — an arrow animates its width and opacity from 0, nudging the label aside as it appears.
  7. Animated gradient — a wide gradient (background-size: 300%) whose background-position drifts on hover, so the colors flow.
  8. Neon glow — layered box-shadow plus text-shadow, with a looping @keyframes pulse while hovered. Cyberpunk in eight lines.
  9. Magnetic — now we need JavaScript. A Svelte {@attach} tracks the pointer and writes --mx/--my custom properties; a springy transition does the rest. The button leans toward your cursor and springs back.
  10. Particle burst — the showpiece. On click, an attachment spawns a ring of dot elements and animates them outward with the Web Animations API, removing each when it finishes. Pure delight, and it cleans up after itself.
One base class, many modifiers. Notice the architecture: a shared .btn holds the padding, radius and isolation: isolate (so ::before layers stack predictably), and each .fx-* class adds just its effect. That's how design systems ship a dozen button styles without a dozen copies — and how you keep them consistent. Lift the whole <style> block into your app and you have a button kit.
Effects are seasoning, not the meal. A particle burst on your primary "Buy now" is delightful; ten different effects on one screen is chaos. Pick one signature interaction for primary actions and keep secondary buttons calm. And every effect here collapses gracefully under prefers-reduced-motion — the buttons still work, they just stop moving.
