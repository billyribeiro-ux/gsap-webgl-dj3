The bespoke cursor is the calling card of high-end portfolio and agency sites. Done well it's three ideas working together: a precise dot that follows the pointer exactly, a larger ring that lags behind with inertia, and a magnetic field where interactive elements pull toward the cursor as it nears them. We scope ours to a single area so it never fights the rest of the page.

The mechanics

  • Two followers, two speeds. The dot is set to the pointer position every frame (instant). The ring eases toward it with current += (target - current) * 0.2 — that lerp is the "weight." Two elements, two follow speeds, and the cursor suddenly feels physical.
  • Snap to targets. When the pointer is over a .target, the ring's goal switches from the pointer to the target's center and it scales up — so it appears to lock onto the button. This is the "I can click this" affordance, made delightful.
  • Magnetic lean. The hovered target itself translates a fraction of the way toward the pointer ((pointer - center) * 0.3), with a springy CSS transition so it glides back on exit. Cursor and target reach for each other.
Don't hide the real cursor everywhere. Setting cursor: none globally is an accessibility and usability risk — touch users get nothing, and a laggy custom cursor on a whole page feels broken if anything stutters. Scope it to a hero or a gallery (as here), keep a fallback, and make sure keyboard focus styles are still excellent. A custom cursor is a flourish for pointer users, never a replacement for accessible interaction.

From this base you can branch endlessly: invert the cursor's colour over images with mix-blend-mode: difference, morph the ring into a "view" or "drag" label over specific elements, leave a fading trail of dots, or distort a WebGL scene at the cursor's position (you built exactly that in the distortion lesson). The cursor is a canvas of its own.
