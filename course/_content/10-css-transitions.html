A transition is the simplest animation there is: "when this property changes, don't snap to the new value — glide there over time." Four words define it — property, duration, easing, delay — and that's genuinely most of what you need to make an interface feel alive.

The magnetic call-to-action

Move your pointer over the button. It leans toward your cursor, a light sweep crosses its surface, and on leave it springs back. Three transitions, one tiny Svelte action — and it feels like something Stripe or Linear would ship.

Anatomy of the transition

The single most important line in that component is this:

transition: transform 0.45s cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1);

  • transform — we animate only transform and opacity. Both are composited on the GPU, so we stay at a buttery 60fps no matter what.
  • 0.45s — long enough to read as deliberate, short enough to feel responsive. This is the "premium" pocket.
  • the cubic-bezier — our expo-out curve. Fast to react, slow to settle. It's why the spring-back feels physical, not mechanical.
Why an attachment? The pointer-tracking is imperative DOM work — exactly what {@attach} exists for. Attachments are Svelte 5's modern successor to actions: they run when the element mounts and return a cleanup function, so the listeners are removed automatically when the button leaves the page. No leaks, no onMount/onDestroy boilerplate. Because magnetic() is just a function, you can attach it — with any strength — to any element in your app.

Transition the change, not the element

A common beginner mistake is thinking "I need to animate this button." You don't. You declare how changes are eased on the element, then change whatever you like — a class, a hover state, a CSS variable — and CSS interpolates for you. That mental shift (declare the easing once, mutate freely) is what makes CSS motion so productive. GSAP, which we meet soon, takes the opposite, imperative approach — and you'll learn exactly when each one wins.
