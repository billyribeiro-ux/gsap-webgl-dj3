Beautiful motion that drops frames feels broken. The good news: hitting a steady 60fps (or 120 on modern displays) is mostly about animating the right properties — and being a responsible engineer means motion that respects the people using it.

Animate transform & opacity. Almost nothing else.

The browser renders in stages: layout (where things go) → paint (how they look) → composite (layering it onto the screen). Animating transform and opacity touches only the last, GPU-accelerated stage. Animating width, height, top/left, or margin forces layout on every single frame — the most expensive work there is — and your framerate collapses.

  • Move things with transform: translate(), not top/left.
  • Resize with transform: scale(), not width/height.
  • Fade with opacity, not by animating colors with alpha.

Reduced motion is non-negotiable

Some people get dizzy or nauseous from motion. Their operating system lets them ask for less of it, and your job is to listen. The demo below flies a panel in — but if you've enabled "reduce motion," it simply appears, instantly.

Two ways to honor the preference. In CSS, wrap motion in @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) and zero out the durations (every component in this course already does this). In Svelte logic, read the reactive prefersReducedMotion.current from svelte/motion and adapt your transition options — exactly what the panel does. Never remove the content; just remove the movement.

A few more pro habits

  • will-change is a scalpel, not a hammer. It promotes an element to its own GPU layer. Add it right before an animation and remove it after; slapping it on everything hurts performance by exhausting GPU memory.
  • Animate fewer, bigger things. Staggering 500 elements is gorgeous but can choke a low-end phone. Test on real, cheap hardware.
  • Clean up. Kill timelines, disconnect observers, dispose WebGL contexts on unmount — the gsap.context / attachment-cleanup pattern from this course handles it automatically.
